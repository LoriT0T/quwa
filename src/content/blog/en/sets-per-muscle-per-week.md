Roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week is where most of the useful evidence sits.
That is a wide range because the right answer genuinely depends on you.

## What counts as a hard set

A set taken to within about three reps of failure, on an exercise that meaningfully loads the
muscle in question. Warm-ups do not count. Neither does a set of lateral raises you stopped
because your phone buzzed.

Counting indirect work is where most people go wrong. Rows train your biceps. Pressing trains your
triceps. If you tally 18 direct sets for biceps and you are also doing 16 sets of rowing, your
biceps are seeing considerably more than 18.

## Start low

If you are unsure, start at 10 sets per muscle per week and add. Two reasons.

First, the dose-response curve is fairly flat in the middle. The difference between 12 and 18 sets
is smaller than the difference between 4 and 12, so there is little cost to starting conservatively.

Second, volume you cannot recover from does not just fail to help — it interferes with everything
else you are doing that week.

## Signs you are above your ceiling

- Performance drops session to session on the same exercise
- Joints ache in ways that do not resolve within a day
- You dread specific sessions
- Sleep quality falls while nothing else has changed
- Progress stalls on a muscle group while other groups keep moving

The last one is the most useful, because it separates a volume problem from a general life problem.

## Training age moves the number

A beginner grows on almost nothing — 6 to 10 sets a week produces real results, and more mostly
produces more soreness. Someone with five years of training may need 16 to 20 sets to keep making
progress on a lagging muscle.

This is why copying an advanced lifter's program is a poor idea in your first two years. It is not
that it is too hard. It is that the volume is calibrated for a body that has had years to build the
work capacity to handle it.

## Periodise it

Volume should not be a constant. A workable pattern is to start a block at the low end, add roughly
two sets per muscle every couple of weeks, and then take a deload week where volume drops by
around half. Then start the next block slightly above where the last one began.

That way the number moves within a range you can sustain, rather than only ever going up until
something forces it back down.
