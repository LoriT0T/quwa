A deload is a planned week of reduced training stress, scheduled before you need it rather than
after you break.

## What to reduce

Not everything. Deloads work best when you cut one variable substantially and leave the others alone.

**Cut volume, keep load.** Same weights, roughly half the sets. This is the default and it works
for most people, because it maintains the neural side of the lifts while giving the connective
tissue and the central nervous system a break.

**Cut load, keep volume.** Same sets, around 60% of the weights. Better if your joints are the
thing complaining rather than your energy.

**Cut both slightly.** Around 70% load, around 60% volume. Appropriate after a very hard block.

What does not work well is taking the week completely off. You lose some of the training effect
and you often come back feeling worse, not better, because a week of nothing does not resolve the
underlying accumulated fatigue any faster than a light week does.

## When to schedule one

Every fourth to sixth week for most people running a structured block. Advanced lifters running
high volume tend toward every fourth week; beginners can often go eight or more, because they are
not generating enough fatigue to need it sooner.

The programs on this site have deload weeks written into the plan, at weeks 4, 8 and 12.

## Signs you needed one two weeks ago

- Loads that felt fine last month now feel heavy on the warm-up sets
- Motivation to train drops without a corresponding life change
- Sleep gets worse or lighter
- Small joint niggles that used to resolve overnight now persist
- Resting heart rate creeps up by several beats

None of these on its own means much. Three of them at once means take the week.

## The psychological part is the hard part

Most lifters who skip deloads do so because a light week feels like wasted time. It is not. The
adaptation happens during recovery, not during the session — training is the stimulus, recovery is
where the change gets made.

Scheduling it in advance removes the decision. That is why it belongs in the program rather than in
your judgement on a Monday morning when you feel fine.
