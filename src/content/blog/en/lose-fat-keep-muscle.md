Some muscle loss during a diet is normal. How much depends mostly on four things, and three of
them you control.

## 1. The size of the deficit

A large deficit loses weight faster and loses more of that weight as muscle. A moderate deficit —
roughly 20% below maintenance — is a reasonable default for most people.

Set it as a percentage rather than a flat number. A 500 kcal deficit is a quarter of a small
person's intake and a sixth of a large person's. Start with the
[TDEE calculator](/en/tools/tdee), take 20% off, and adjust from what the scale actually does over
a fortnight.

## 2. Protein

This is the highest-leverage variable. In a deficit, push toward 2.0–2.4 g per kilogram of body
weight. Higher than a maintenance phase, because protein does more work when the rest of your
intake is down: it preserves lean tissue and it keeps you fuller.

The [protein calculator](/en/tools/protein) adjusts for goal automatically.

## 3. Resistance training — and specifically, keeping the load

This is where most people get it backwards. Cutting calories, they also drop the weights and add
reps "to burn more". That is the wrong order.

The signal that tells your body to keep muscle is mechanical tension — heavy loads. So in a
deficit, hold the weights and cut the volume. Fewer sets, same bar. Our
[Lean Recomposition](/en/programs/lean-recomposition) block is written around exactly that.

Cardio is a fine tool for creating part of the deficit. It is not a replacement for lifting, and
it does nothing to signal muscle retention.

## 4. How lean you already are

This is the one you do not control. The leaner you get, the more aggressively your body defends
remaining fat and the more readily it gives up muscle. Someone at 30% body fat has an easy time
of it. Someone at 12% does not.

This also means recomposition — gaining muscle while losing fat — is realistic for beginners,
returners and people carrying more fat, and increasingly unrealistic for lean, trained people.
That is not a motivational statement, it is just how the arithmetic works.

## Rate to aim for

Roughly 0.5–1% of body weight per week. Faster than that and the proportion coming from lean tissue
climbs sharply.

## When to stop

Diets work better in blocks than as a permanent state. Ten to sixteen weeks, then a period at
maintenance. Adherence falls over time, and a diet you abandon in week twenty produces a worse
result than one you finish in week twelve.
