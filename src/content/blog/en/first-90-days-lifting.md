Your first year of training produces the fastest results you will ever get. It is also the year
most people waste, by following programs written for people ten years ahead of them.

## Weeks 1–4: learn the movements

Three full-body sessions a week. Six to eight exercises. Two to three sets each, stopping several
reps short of failure.

That will feel too easy. Do it anyway. In the first month your nervous system is learning the
movement patterns, and adding load faster than technique improves is how people acquire the habits
they spend year two undoing.

Pick one squat pattern, one hinge, one press, one pull, and something for the core. That is a
complete program.

## Weeks 5–8: start adding load

Now push closer to failure — one to two reps in reserve on your working sets — and add weight when
you can complete every set at the top of the rep range.

Write down every set. This is the habit that separates people who progress from people who train.
You cannot progressively overload something you are not recording.

## Weeks 9–12: add a little structure

Move to four days if your schedule allows: two upper, two lower. Add a second exercise per movement
pattern. Keep the same progression rule.

By the end of week 12 you should be noticeably stronger on every lift, and you should have twelve
weeks of written data telling you exactly how much.

## The three expensive mistakes

**Programme hopping.** Changing every three weeks because progress feels slow. Nothing has time to
work. Pick one thing and run it for twelve weeks.

**Chasing soreness.** Soreness measures novelty, not effectiveness. A session that leaves you
destroyed is not better than one that leaves you able to train again on Thursday.

**Ignoring protein.** This is the single highest-return nutrition change a beginner can make, and
it is free if you already eat enough calories. Run the numbers with the
[protein calculator](/en/tools/protein) and the [TDEE calculator](/en/tools/tdee).

## What to ignore entirely for now

Supplements beyond protein powder and possibly creatine. Advanced intensity techniques. Fasted
training. Anything described as "hacks". Split debates. Body-part specialisation.

None of it matters until the basics have been consistent for a year, and all of it is a way of
feeling productive without being productive.
