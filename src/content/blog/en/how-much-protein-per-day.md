Around 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of body weight per day, if you train with weights. That is
the range the evidence supports, and most of the argument beyond it is marketing.

## Where the range comes from

The often-cited figure is a meta-analysis finding that the benefits of additional protein for
muscle growth flatten out at roughly 1.6 g/kg, with the confidence interval extending to about
2.2 g/kg. That upper end is where the "eat more to be safe" advice sensibly stops.

Going above it is not dangerous for people with healthy kidneys. It is simply not more useful,
and it costs money and displaces other food.

## Cutting changes the number

In a calorie deficit, protein does more work: it helps preserve muscle while you are under-fuelled,
and it is the most satiating macronutrient, which matters when you are hungry. Pushing toward
2.0–2.4 g/kg during a deficit is reasonable, and our [protein calculator](/en/tools/protein)
adjusts for goal automatically.

## Per meal matters more than per hour

The "anabolic window" — the idea that you must consume protein within an hour of training — has
not held up. Total daily intake is the dominant variable.

What does matter is distribution. Getting a reasonable dose in each of three to five meals appears
to produce a better response than getting the same total in one or two. Roughly 0.4 g/kg per meal
is a workable rule, which for an 80 kg lifter is about 30 g per meal across four meals.

## Which protein

For most people, the source is less important than the total. Animal sources are more complete and
more leucine-dense, so they hit the threshold with a smaller dose. Plant sources work perfectly
well when total intake is adequate and sources are varied — you simply need a slightly larger dose
to reach the same leucine content.

Powder is food, not a supplement. It is convenient and cheap per gram, and there is nothing
special about it beyond that.

## The honest summary

Work out your range, hit the middle of it most days, spread it across your meals, and stop
thinking about it. Protein is the easiest variable in nutrition to get right and the one people
spend the most time worrying about after they already have.
