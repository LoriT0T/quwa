Progressive overload means the demand on the muscle increases over time. Almost everyone reduces
this to "add 2.5 kg", which works for about six months and then stops.

## The five variables

**Load.** More weight for the same reps. The most obvious, and the one that runs out first.

**Reps.** More reps at the same weight. Usually how you get to a load increase — you climb the
rep range, then add weight and drop back to the bottom of it.

**Sets.** More total work per session or per week. Effective, but it is the variable most likely
to outrun your recovery, so it should climb slowly and come back down periodically.

**Range of motion.** A deeper squat is a harder squat at the same load. This one is free, and most
lifters have a lot of it available.

**Control.** Slower eccentrics, a pause at the hardest position, less momentum. A set of ten with
a two-second lowering phase is a substantially harder set than ten sloppy reps at the same weight.

## Why the rep range exists

This is the mechanism that makes progression work when load alone stops. Given a target of 3 sets
of 8–12:

- Week 1: 60 kg × 8, 8, 8
- Week 2: 60 kg × 10, 9, 8
- Week 3: 60 kg × 12, 11, 10
- Week 4: 60 kg × 12, 12, 12 → all sets at the top, so next week the load goes to 65 kg
- Week 5: 65 kg × 8, 8, 7

Nothing went up in week 5 on paper. Everything went up over the block. That is progressive
overload working correctly, and it is why judging a program by one session is useless.

## When you stall

A stall is not a failure of effort. It is information. In order, check:

1. **Sleep.** Nothing else on this list matters if this one is broken.
2. **Calories.** Progress in a deficit is slower by design. If you are cutting, holding your loads
   is a good outcome, not a stall.
3. **Recovery volume.** If your weekly sets for that muscle have crept up for six weeks, they are
   probably now above what you can recover from.
4. **Technique drift.** Compare a video with one from eight weeks ago. Range of motion quietly
   shrinks as loads climb, and a shorter rep is an easier rep.
5. **Actual programming.** Only after the first four.

## Track it or it did not happen

You cannot progressively overload something you are not recording. Write down the weight and the
reps for every working set. Use the [one-rep max calculator](/en/tools/one-rep-max) to convert a
set into a comparable number when the rep count changes between weeks.

Memory is not a training log. It is a story you tell yourself about a training log.
