Creatine monohydrate is the most researched supplement in sports nutrition, with hundreds of
controlled trials behind it. That makes it unusual: most of what is sold to lifters has nothing
like this evidence base.

## What it does

It increases the phosphocreatine stored in your muscle, which is the energy system your body uses
for short, high-intensity efforts. Practically, that means a rep or two more on a heavy set, and
slightly better recovery between sets.

Over months, that additional training volume compounds into more strength and more muscle. The
effect is real and it is modest — on the order of a few extra percent, not a transformation.

## What it does not do

It is not a hormone and it is not related to anabolic steroids. It does not damage kidneys in
people with healthy kidney function — this has been examined directly and repeatedly. It does not
cause hair loss; one small study found a change in a hormone marker and it has never been
replicated as actual hair loss.

It does not cause bloating in the sense people mean. It draws water **into** muscle cells, which
is where you want it. Some people gain one to two kilograms of scale weight in the first fortnight
from intramuscular water. That is not fat and it is not subcutaneous.

## How to take it

Three to five grams a day, every day, at any time. That is the entire protocol.

Loading — 20 g a day for five days — saturates your stores faster, in about a week rather than
about a month. It is optional and it makes some people feel unwell. There is no long-term
difference between loading and not loading.

Timing does not matter. Neither does taking it with carbohydrate, taking it before training, or
cycling off it.

## Which form

Monohydrate. Every other form is more expensive and none has beaten it in a head-to-head trial.
"Micronised" is monohydrate ground finer, which helps it dissolve and does nothing else.

Look for a product that carries third-party testing, since supplements are not tightly regulated in
most markets.

## Who responds

Roughly 20–30% of people are non-responders, usually because their muscle creatine stores are
already high — often people who eat a lot of red meat and fish. If you take it consistently for six
weeks and notice nothing, you may be one of them.

It is one of the few supplements worth the money. It is also worth considerably less than getting
your sleep, your protein and your training consistency right first.
