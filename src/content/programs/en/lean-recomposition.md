## Cut volume, keep load

In a deficit you recover less well, so something in your training has to give. The evidence is
reasonably clear about which: keeping the bar heavy and reducing how much total work you do
preserves strength and muscle better than the opposite.

Most people do the opposite. They drop the weights and add reps, "to burn more", and lose more
muscle than they needed to.

## The nutrition part is a framework, not a menu

You get a protein target, a calorie target derived from your own maintenance, and a fibre and
fat floor. You do not get a meal plan, because meal plans are abandoned in week three by almost
everyone who is not being paid to follow one.

## Recomposition is not a promise

Gaining muscle and losing fat at the same time happens most reliably in people who are new to
lifting, returning after a break, or carrying more body fat. If you are lean and trained, expect
this block to hold what you have while fat comes off. That is the honest version.
