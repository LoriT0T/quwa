## Why this block is built the way it is

Most hypertrophy programs give you a spreadsheet and leave the reasoning out. That is a problem
the first time your gym is busy, your shoulder aches, or a week goes badly — because without the
reasoning you cannot adapt, so you either follow it rigidly through a session that is not working
or abandon it entirely.

This block runs three four-week phases. Volume climbs across each phase, then drops in the fourth
week. Loads climb across the whole twelve weeks. That is the entire structure, and the rationale
page explains why those two variables move on different timescales.

## Progression you can measure

Each exercise carries a rep target and a rep range. When you hit the top of the range on every
set, the load goes up next session. When you fall below the bottom, it comes down. There is no
percentage-of-max maths and nothing to estimate mid-session — you write down what you lifted, and
the sheet tells you what to do next week.

## What this will not do

It will not make you strong on a competition squat, bench and deadlift. Strength Base does that.
It will not work well on three days a week without modification, and it assumes you can train
around minor niggles rather than needing a rehab protocol.
