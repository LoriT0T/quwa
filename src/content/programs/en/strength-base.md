## Base first, then peak

A peak makes you express strength you already have. It does not build much. So this block spends
ten weeks building — moderate loads, high-ish volume, technical work on the positions where most
lifters fail — and only then spends six weeks converting that into a heavier single.

Running a peak without a base is the most common reason a strength block produces a smaller number
than the lifter expected.

## Autoregulation, explained rather than assumed

Every heavy day opens with a top set taken to a target effort rather than a target percentage.
On a good day that is a heavier bar; on a bad day it is a lighter one, and the back-off sets
follow from whatever the top set actually was.

This is the single feature that makes a sixteen-week block survivable. Fixed percentages assume
every week goes to plan, and they do not.

## The test week

Week sixteen is a test, and the protocol is written out: the warm-up ladder, the attempt
selection, the rest periods, and what to do if the second attempt moves badly.
